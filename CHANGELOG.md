## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added
- F-27 (Gobber 2 2.8.9, optional, both sides): the dragon armour sends the player's abilities only when they changed, so its creative flight holds on a server. Gobber's dragon armour tick set the "may fly" flag and called `Player.onUpdateAbilities()` every tick for every piece worn, with no side check; Forge runs the armour tick from the inventory tick on the client and on the server, so with the full set on the server sent four abilities packets a tick and the client four back, and vanilla's two handlers overwrite the `flying` flag with whatever the other side last sent (harmless in vanilla, which only sends on a change). With any latency the flag became a loop with no damping: the double tap turned flight on for one tick in every round trip, the player vibrated and flight never held (fine in single player; Gobber's open issues #14 and #25, where players roll back to 2.8.7). The mixin remembers the two flags at the head of the tick and redirects its `onUpdateAbilities()` calls: on the server the update goes out only when `mayfly` or `flying` changed in that call (the grant when the set is completed, the revoke when a piece comes off), on the client never (the double tap sends its own packet). `fixDragonFlight` in `bsp_mod_fixes-server.toml` (default on, synced to clients) turns the rule off and lets every call through as Gobber makes it, for the day Gobber fixes it itself. The mixin applies only when exactly Gobber 2 2.8.9 is loaded and its dragon armour class still has the patched method, and is skipped with one WARN line otherwise.
- `./gradlew <run> -PwithGobber` loads Gobber 2 (the exact pack file) in a dev run, so the dragon armour test ticks a dressed player through the real item.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-07

### Added
- F-24 (Modular Powersuits 2.3.13): the Glider, Sprint Assist and Jump Assist modules leave the player's creative flying speed as they found it. The Glider added 0.03 to vanilla's `Abilities.flyingSpeed` every tick the player glided, and the two assist modules set it to a fifth of the walking speed every tick they ran; none of them restored it, and Powersuits' own flight never reads it, so the writes did nothing for a suit wearer. But vanilla saves that value with the player (`abilities.flySpeed`), sends it to the client with every abilities update, and BSP-InventorySync carries it across the network; the moment anything granted creative flight (AdvancedAE's Flight card) the player flew at whatever was left behind, 0.65 after one second of gliding being 144 blocks a second, and Gotta Go Fast's raised speed limit let it through. Three mixins remember the speed at the head of each module's tick and put it back at its return. A guard on the server tick puts a survival or adventure player's stored speed back to vanilla's 0.05 once a second when it is anything else, with one log line per reset, so every player already affected heals on their next tick; creative and spectator players are left alone; `keepFlyingSpeed` in `bsp_mod_fixes-server.toml` turns the guard off.
- F-25 (AdvancedAE 1.3.6-1.20.1, optional, both sides): the quantum armor's damage absorption is shown on a suit readout. AdvancedAE takes a share of every hit a player receives (after armor, enchantments and absorption hearts) out of the four pieces' AE charge, a quarter each at 1,000 AE per damage point, and says nothing; against the armor's own 80 % reduction and a buffer of hundreds of millions of AE nobody could see it work. AdvancedAE's handler is replaced by one with the same arithmetic and rules (the percentage from its config, the price, the cancel of a hit that ends under one point) that sends the wearer one packet per absorption. On the client a plate slides in at the screen's left edge above the hotbar while any quantum piece is worn: the suit's charge as a gold bar with its percentage and figure, and for three seconds after a hit a damage row with the hit after the armor, the share taken off in teal, "0.6/2.0", the slice paid as a notch on the charge bar and "-600 AE" floating off the plate. A tab on the plate's right edge collapses it off screen to the tab alone (its arrow then points the other way, with a sliver of the charge), by the key binding "Toggle quantum suit readout" (unbound by default) or a click on the tab while a container screen (the inventory, a chest) is open and nothing of that screen is under the cursor; the collapsed state is kept in `bsp_mod_fixes-client.toml` until changed again. The mixin applies only when exactly AdvancedAE 1.3.6-1.20.1 is loaded and is skipped with one WARN line otherwise; the readout is installed on a client only then. The readout's labels and key names are this jar's own lang file, `assets/bsp_mod_fixes/lang/en_us.json`, written by `tools/gen_lang.py`. A network channel of this mod's own, `bsp_mod_fixes:main`, carries the packet.
- F-26 (Modular Powersuits 2.3.13, client only): Powersuits' HUD energy meter shows only while a Powersuits modular item is worn or held. Powersuits drew its meters on every HUD frame whenever Numina's sum of the player's energy was above zero, and that sum takes the Forge energy capability of every equipped item of any mod, so anyone wearing energy-storing armor (AdvancedAE's quantum set exposes its AE as FE) got Powersuits' meter with no Powersuits item on. The meter overlay's draw is cancelled unless a modular item is in an equipment slot, the rule Powersuits already uses for the rest of its HUD. The mixin targets a client-only class and is skipped on a dedicated server.
- `./gradlew <run> -PwithAae` loads AdvancedAE, AE2, GuideME and GeckoLib (the exact pack files) in a dev run; `-PskipFixes=<mixin names>` switches named mixins off for a run, so a test can show the patched mod's own behaviour.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-06

### Added
- F-19 (JEI 15.74, client only): JEI starts only after the server's tags have arrived. At login Minecraft 1.20.1 sends the recipe packet before the tag packet, and JEI 15.74's start observer starts JEI inside the recipe packet's handler without waiting for the tag sync, so every JEI plugin that resolves a tag ingredient while registering its recipes saw an empty tag on a dedicated server: Mekanism dropped such recipes as incomplete (every Metallurgic Infuser recipe, the alloys and steel among them, and most of its ore processing: 1,212 of the pack's 4,711 Mekanism recipes), Voltaic's categories threw on them (83 Electrodynamics, Nuclear Science, Blastcraft and JAOPCA fluid recipes, "Found a broken recipe" in the log), and other plugins showed Forge's "Empty Tag" barrier. Single player was unaffected, because the integrated server binds the tags before either packet. The mixin cancels JEI's `startIfReady` while the current login's tag packet has not been handled and has it run again on the next client tick once it has (one INFO line each), remembered per login (the client's packet listener, new for every login and every Velocity switch) so each login holds again. JEI is an optional client-side dependency; the mixin applies to any JEI 15.74 build whose start observer still has the two patched methods, and is skipped with one WARN line otherwise, and on a dedicated server.
- `./gradlew runClient -PwithMekanism`, `-PwithAu`, `-PwithCrt` load the pack's Mekanism, AlmostUnified, CraftTweaker and JEITweaker in a dev run, and `-PrecipeProbe` runs a dev-only probe that logs what JEI and EMI hold for Mekanism once a world is open (never in the jar).

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-06

### Added
- F-16 (Minecraft 1.20.1): a packet sent to the server just before a server switch moved the connection to another protocol is dropped instead of closing the connection. Vanilla's `Connection.sendPacket` reads the channel's protocol when a packet is sent and, off the netty thread, hands `doSendPacket` the protocol it read; `doSendPacket` switches the channel's protocol only when the packet's protocol differs from that reading. During a Velocity switch with Client Reset Packet the netty thread moves the channel to LOGIN while the world unloads (three to thirteen seconds on the pack), so a PLAY packet a client mod sent from the render thread in that window reached the encoder labelled PLAY on PLAY, nothing switched the protocol, and the encoder threw "Can't serialize unregistered packet", which closed the connection the instant the reset completed ("Failed to connect to the server, Internal Exception: io.netty.handler.codec.EncoderException"). Such a packet is now dropped with one WARN line, `[BSP Mod Fixes] Dropped a stale PLAY packet (<class>[ on channel <id>]) after a server switch; the connection is now in LOGIN`, naming the packet class and, for a custom payload, its channel, so the mod that sent it can be found. A send that carries its own protocol change (the handshake intention packet) is untouched, and so are a server's connections: the mixin is in the jar on both sides and inert on servers.
- F-17 (Minecraft 1.20.1, client only): a disconnected screen set from any thread but the render thread is opened on the render thread instead. Client Reset Packet 0.3.2 overwrites `Connection.disconnect` on the client and sets a `DisconnectedScreen` from the netty thread when a switch fails; `Minecraft.setScreen` assigns the screen before initialising it, so the render thread could draw it uninitialised, and with the Fabric Screen API (Forgified Fabric API, pulled in by Enhanced Block Entities) that was a crash, "The current screen (DisconnectedScreen) has not been correctly initialised". The same call is re-submitted through `Minecraft.execute` and the one in hand cancelled, with one INFO line naming the thread; every other screen is left to vanilla. The mixin targets a client-only class, so the mixin config plugin skips it on a dedicated server and the startup report lists it as skipped.
- The mixin config plugin gates mixins into client-only Minecraft classes by the loader's dist (`ClientGate`), the way the EMI mixins are gated by EMI's presence; a mixin that targets Minecraft is remapped for production like any other mod's (the refmap carries the methods, the shadowed fields are renamed at reobfuscation).

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-04

### Added
- EMI support, gated: the fixes for EMI's JEI compatibility layer (F-11 onward) apply only when EMI 1.1.24+1.20.1+forge is loaded. EMI is an optional client-side dependency; without it the mixins are skipped before Mixin looks at their targets, and with any other EMI version one WARN line names the version found and the fixes are skipped. The startup report lists skipped fixes with the reason.
- F-11 (EMI): a null entry in a JEI ingredient list, which JEI's API allows and JEI's own layout builder skips, converts to the empty EMI stack instead of throwing. EMI's `JemiUtil.getStack` handed the null to `EmiStack.of`, which dropped the whole recipe at reload with "Exception thrown adding adding JEI recipe"; Tinkers' Construct's entity melting and severing categories pass null for every entity without a spawn egg, so those categories were empty in EMI.
- F-12 (EMI): EMI's JEI tooltip builder gains the four JEI 15.39-15.52 methods it never implemented: `getLines()` (a live list over EMI's own lines, so removing or adding through it changes what EMI renders), `clear()`, `clearIngredient()` and `addKeyUsageComponent()` (JEI's form: the key name in bold on an italic gray line). EMI compiled against JEI 15.20 and these are abstract in the JEI API, so any JEI plugin calling one of them under EMI threw `AbstractMethodError`; Tinkers' Construct calls `getLines()` for every fluid slot, which turned the whole recipe into "Error Rendering" with the stack trace as the tooltip.
- F-13 (EMI): a fluid's amount line under a JEI rich tooltip callback is offered to the callback under JEI's key `jei.tooltip.liquid.amount`. Tinkers' Construct removes that line and adds Mantle's unit lines ("1 Ingot"), which under EMI left both EMI's "1,000mB" and Tinkers' units, the latter after the mod name; now the units take the amount line's place, as in JEI. A callback that leaves the line alone gets EMI's line back where it was.
- F-14 (EMI): EMI's JEI recipe extras builder gains the JEI 15.56 widget methods it lacked (`addDrawableWidget`, `addTooltipArea`, `addRecipeArrowWidget`, `addRecipePlusSignWidget`, `addAnimatedRecipeArrowWidget`, `addAnimatedRecipeFlameWidget`, `addButton`, `addCycleButton`) and every EMI placeable gains the aligned `setPosition` overload. Each widget is drawn with EMI's own widgets the way EMI's older `addDrawable` and `addRecipeArrow` are, and shows the tooltip the plugin set (text, lines, a rich component or a callback) over its area. Tinkers' Construct draws its arrows, icons and tooltip areas through these, so every Tinkers category's extras threw `AbstractMethodError` under EMI ("Exception adding JEMI extras" in the log) and the recipe drew without arrows. Buttons are minimal working widgets (a bordered box with the message or icon; a click runs the action, a cycle button steps its values).
- F-15 (EMI): JEI text widgets are drawn in EMI. EMI's `addText` returned a widget it never added to its widget list, so JEI plugins' titles and labels (Tinkers' temperatures, cooling times, recipe titles) never showed under EMI. They are now wrapped to their width, aligned as JEI aligns them, with JEI's default line spacing, and take the four `setTooltip` forms and the two `setPosition` forms JEI 15.56 declares on `ITextWidget` (JEI redeclares `setPosition` with the `ITextWidget` return type, which EMI's widget never got, so `addText(...).setPosition(x, y)` threw `AbstractMethodError` as well).
- `./gradlew runClient -PquickPlay=<world>` opens a world in `run/saves` at once, so a headless run reaches EMI's recipe reload.
- `./gradlew <run> -PwithEmi` loads EMI, JEI, Tinkers' Construct and Mantle in a dev run (the exact pack files, from CurseMaven) for the in-game checks; without it the runs stay EMI-free, which is what the headless tests check.

### Changed
- F-6: the Kinetic Generator's position map is touched by the server thread only. In single player the client's player shares its entity id with the server's, so the client tick could evict the server's entry on every jump and lose a tick's credit (audit finding).
- F-1 and F-2: the refusal and clamp WARN lines are throttled to one per player every 10 seconds, with the count of lines held back, so a hostile client cannot flood the server log (audit finding).
- The Forge range is `[47.3.10,)`: Forge began handing the loading context to mod constructors in 47.3.10, and older 47.x would have refused the mod at load despite the old `[47,)`.
- `./gradlew check` skips the Python lang check with a warning when `python3` is not on the PATH (the lang GameTest covers the same files).
- The startup report sorts fix ids tolerantly (an id without a number no longer breaks the report).
- `docs/CONFIG.md`: a note on the Kinetic Generator's whole-FE truncation and its one-block-per-tick ceiling.
- F-13 also covers a slot holding several fluids (a tag-backed input, as in alloying): EMI shows the fluid it picked for the second, and its amount line is now found there too (audit finding).
- F-12: a tooltip component the client has no renderer for becomes an empty line instead of being dropped from the live list, so list iterators keep their place (audit finding).
- F-15: text widgets start without a shadow, as JEI's do (EMI's started with one), and when an area cannot hold all the lines the full text goes into the widget's tooltip, as JEI does (audit findings).
- The EMI fixes are reported as skipped on a dedicated server ("client-side only") instead of waiting for targets that never load there (audit finding).

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-04

### Added
- The project: a Forge 1.20.1 mod that requires Numina 2.3.13 and Modular Powersuits 2.3.13 exactly, on both sides, with a mixin setup for patching them, a startup log of the fixes active in the build, and the headless test harness.
- F-1 (Modular Powersuits): the creative-install packet, which puts a fully charged module into a worn modular item, is refused unless the sender is in creative mode. Powersuits' handler never checked the game mode, so a modified client could install any module in survival. A refused request is logged at WARN with the player's name.
- F-2 (Numina): a module tweak request is clamped to 0..1 before the server stores it; NaN and infinities become 0. The stock slider only sends 0..1 and Numina stored any double unchecked, which the modules' linear properties then multiplied. A clamped request is logged at WARN with the player's name.
- F-3 (Numina): the player tick handler that ticks every worn module runs once per tick, on the START phase. It never checked the phase, and Forge fires the event twice a tick, so jet thrust, module drains and heat cooling were all doubled. Expect jetpack and jet-boots thrust to halve; the pack's server config is re-tuned for that separately.
- F-4 (Modular Powersuits): the player update handler that clamps fall distance and manages suit sounds runs on the living tick only. It subscribed to the base living event, so it ran for every hurt, jump, fall and drop event of every living entity.
- F-5 (Modular Powersuits): Night Vision refreshes its effect when under 220 ticks remain, with a 400-tick effect, so it charges its 100 FE about every 9 seconds. It refreshed when under 500 ticks remained but applied a 300-tick effect, so it charged every tick.
- F-6 (Modular Powersuits): the Kinetic Generator pays its energy-per-block rate for the horizontal distance walked each tick, on the server, and nothing while flying, riding, gliding or airborne, or for a step over one block (a teleport). It sampled one tick in twenty, only when the player was on the ground at that tick, and its "not generating" branch fell through and generated anyway. The pack's rate is re-tuned for real blocks in the server config notes.
- F-7 (Modular Powersuits): the Shock Absorber absorbs the share of a fall its slider sets (0 to 100 %), pays its energy cost per block absorbed, and when the battery holds less it absorbs the part it can pay for. It was all or nothing: a battery that could not pay for the whole fall absorbed none of it, and its multiplier ran up to ten times the fall. Falls of three blocks or less are left alone as before. Powersuits' landing sound for the absorber is not played.
- F-8 (Modular Powersuits): the Energy Shield absorbs part of each hit for energy instead of adding armor points: 4 % per shield point as vanilla armor does, 80 % at most across all shields, at the module's FE per damage point; with less energy it absorbs what it can pay for, and damage that bypasses armor is untouched. Powersuits added the shield to the armor attribute, its energy drain was dead code, and its tick switched the module off for good whenever energy dipped under one hit's cost. The knockback resistance stays, and the module stays on when the battery is low.
- F-9 (Numina, Modular Powersuits): English names. Numina ships its lang file as `us_en.json`, which Minecraft never loads, so every Numina name showed as a raw key; this jar ships `assets/numina/lang/en_us.json` with all of it plus the key category, the field-of-view key as the code names it, and the capacitor, liquid nitrogen and plating component names. Powersuits lacked a toggle-key name for every module and a few GUI keys; this jar ships only those. Both files come from `tools/gen_lang.py`, and `./gradlew check` runs `tools/check_lang.py` on them.
- F-10 (Numina), an option: the server config `bsp_mod_fixes-server.toml` has `suitClearsFire` (default `true`). Numina puts out a burning player who wears or holds a modular item while the suit's heat is under 95 % of its maximum, which makes suit wearers all but immune to fire aspect and flame; `false` lets the fire burn.
- `docs/CONFIG.md`: the server config file of this mod and the Powersuits server config values to re-tune after F-3 and F-6.
